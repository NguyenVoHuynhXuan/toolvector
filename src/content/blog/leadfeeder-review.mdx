## Quick Verdict

Leadfeeder is a mature, well-integrated tool for finding out which companies visit your B2B website, and it is one of the better-documented options for teams that must stay on the right side of European privacy rules. It shows company names, pages viewed and traffic sources, scores them against your ideal customer profile, and pushes them into your CRM or Slack. Paid tiers add verified contact data, intent filtering and ad campaigns.

**Best for:** B2B sales and marketing teams, especially in Europe, with steady traffic from their target market and someone who can follow up within a day or two.

**Not ideal for:** low-traffic sites, teams that need the name of the individual visitor, small budgets that cannot absorb a jump from the entry paid tier to the next one, and US-first prospecting.

**Strongest point:** the free tier and 14-day trial let you test identification on your own traffic before spending anything, and the compliance posture is better documented than most rivals. **Biggest weakness:** identification is company-level and limited by how IP matching works, so results vary a lot by audience, and several reviewers complain about pricing and renewals.

### 5 Things to Know Before Buying

1. **It identifies companies, not people.** Leadfeeder resolves visitors to organizations. Finding the right person is a separate step, which needs contact credits on Activate and above.
2. **Match rates vary widely.** The vendor says it can identify up to 45% of visiting companies. One independent reviewer cites about 10% as typical for many teams, and a category-wide roundup puts rigorously verified rates for most providers at 5% to 30%.
3. **Prices are in euros and move in bands.** Paid plans start at €79 per month billed annually for Discover, but cost depends on how many companies you identify, and the jump to Activate (from €369) is large.

## What Is Leadfeeder?

<Image src={pic1} alt="What Is Leadfeeder?" class="prose-img" />

Leadfeeder is website visitor identification software. It combines IP-to-company matching with B2B company and contact data so sales and marketing teams can see which organizations visit their site, what they look at and how warm they are. The page footer names Dealfront Group GmbH as the legal entity.

The history matters because older reviews are confusing. Leadfeeder was a module inside the Dealfront platform for several years. On 24 March 2026 Dealfront was folded back into the Leadfeeder name: logins moved to the Leadfeeder domain, the old Leadfeeder module became "Web Visitors," and the old Promote module became "Campaigns." The vendor describes it as a brand change rather than a contract change, so existing subscriptions, prices and features carried over.

The company says more than 15,000 companies use it and displays a five-star G2 badge with over 850 reviews (both vendor-reported; see Claims We Checked). Independent Capterra data shows 4.2 out of 5 across 136 reviews.

**Who it suits:**
- B2B SaaS, services and manufacturing teams with a defined ideal customer profile
- Marketing teams that want to prove which campaigns bring target accounts
- Sales teams in Europe who value EU-based data and documented compliance
- RevOps teams that want website intent inside Salesforce, HubSpot or Pipedrive

**Who it does not suit:**
- Ecommerce and consumer sites, where visitors are individuals rather than companies
- Teams that need named individuals, as with person-level tools aimed at US traffic
- Very low-traffic sites, where few visits will resolve to useful accounts
- Teams without capacity to follow up quickly, since intent signals decay fast

## Key Features: Deep Dive

Leadfeeder lists many modules. Six account for most of the value.

### 1. Web Visitor Identification
<Image src={pic2} alt="Web Visitor Identification" class="prose-img" />

**What it does.** A JavaScript snippet on your site matches visitor IP addresses to companies, then shows who visited, which pages they viewed, how long they stayed and where they came from. Identified companies appear within hours, and the vendor claims about four minutes from setup to first results. It filters out ISPs and low-quality traffic before counting toward your plan.

**How it works in practice.** A software vendor installs the snippet through Google Tag Manager. By the next morning the dashboard lists organizations that read the pricing page and a competitor-comparison page. A custom feed then limits the view to companies of 50 to 500 employees in the target countries.

**Strengths.**
- Quick setup that an independent reviewer completed in under half an hour, with no developer needed for the basics
- Custom feeds for precise segmentation by size, industry, location and behavior
- The vendor says it also identifies companies whose staff work remotely, citing constant IP database updates

**Limits.**
- Reverse-IP matching has hard limits with mobile, VPN and residential traffic, which grew with remote work. One independent reviewer cites about 10% of actual visitors as typical, against the vendor's claim of up to 45%
- Duplicate company entries occasionally need manual cleanup, according to reviewers
- The Free tier shows identified companies but not what they did on your site, per the vendor's homepage

**Versus competitors.** Albacross and Leadinfo do company-level identification with similar mechanics. Person-level tools such as RB2B go further, but are described as US-only.

**Who benefits most:** B2B teams with at least a few thousand monthly visits from their target market.

### 2. Buying Intent Signals, IP Insights and Leadfeeder AIC
<Image src={pic2} alt="uying Intent Signals, IP Insights and Leadfeeder AIC" class="prose-img" />

**What it does.** Beyond a raw list of visitors, Leadfeeder groups companies showing signs of purchase interest, such as repeat visits or high-value pages. ICP Insights segments visitors by how closely they match your ideal customer profile, and Leadfeeder AI prioritizes leads so follow-up targets the best fits first. Activate and above allow filtering by intent signals.

**Example.** A sales team sets up a view of companies that visited the pricing page twice in a week and match the ICP. Those accounts go to the top of the SDR queue and trigger a Slack alert.

**Strengths.**
- Turns a long list into a short, prioritized queue
- Scoring uses your own criteria, so the logic is easy to inspect

**Limits.**
- One reviewer found the scoring rule-based rather than machine-learning driven
- Intent data sources are limited, according to some Capterra reviewers
- Signal decays fast, so a lead from weeks ago is effectively cold

**Who benefits most:** teams with a defined ICP and a clear handoff from marketing to sales.

### 3. Contact Data, Enrichment and the Browser Extension

**What it does.** Knowing the company is half the job. Activate and above add verified emails and phone numbers, and the vendor claims access to 60 million companies and 400 million verified contacts. Records can be enriched, saved to Lists, or viewed through a browser extension while you browse. Global Account Intelligence displays company and contact data inside your CRM records. These actions use credits.

**How credits behave.** According to one detailed 2026 review, a credit unlocks a record for 12 months, email plus phone costs one credit, email alone costs a fifth of a credit, and a CRM export costs one. Identifying visiting companies does not use credits. See the worked examples in the pricing section.

**Strengths.**
- Contact data lives in the same platform as the visit that triggered the lead
- Company data comes from registers, chambers of commerce and other public sources, which helps with European accuracy

**Limits.**
- Reviewers report that contact reveals sometimes return incomplete or outdated data
- Contact coverage is stronger in Europe than elsewhere. One reviewer cites roughly 26 million company records in Europe against about 8.5 million elsewhere, with weaker SME coverage in the US and Asia-Pacific
- Verified contact data starts at Activate, not Discover

**Who benefits most:** European outbound and account-based teams that want one tool for both signal and contact.

### 4. CRM Integrations and No-Code Workflows
<Image src={pic6} alt="Web Visitor Identification" class="prose-img" />

**What it does.** Leadfeeder syncs with Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho and Microsoft Dynamics, and connects to Slack, Google Analytics 4, Google Ads, Mailchimp and Zapier. Workflows, a newer feature, adds no-code automation for tasks such as inbound routing, CRM cleanup and intent alerts. Activate adds CRM automations, and Scale adds automatic CRM data updates.

**Real-world scenario.** When an ICP-matched company visits the demo page, a workflow creates or updates the account in HubSpot, assigns the owner and posts a message to the sales channel.

**Strengths.**
- Two-way CRM sync worked without heavy configuration in an independent test
- Unlimited users on every plan share one credit pool, so adding teammates costs nothing

**Limits.**
- No built-in email sequencing and no native LinkedIn integration, per one reviewer, so outreach happens elsewhere
- The Zapier connection required generating a personal API token first in that test
- Where your own tool is not on the list, you rely on Zapier or the API

**Who benefits most:** RevOps teams that want website behavior to trigger work automatically.

### 5. Campaigns and Audience Building
<Image src={pic5} alt="Campaigns and Audience Building" class="prose-img" />

**What it does.** Campaigns, formerly called Promote, takes identified accounts and uses them for B2B display advertising and retargeting. The vendor claims it can bring up to three times more ICP companies to your site and cut cost per qualified lead by up to 35%. Audience building uses more than 100 filters and AI enrichment. Display campaigns require Activate or higher.

**Example.** A marketer builds a list of target accounts that have not visited yet, then runs display ads aimed only at those companies and attributes later visits back to the campaign.

**Strengths.** It closes a loop most visitor tools leave open: identify, advertise, then see the same companies return.

**Limits.** The performance figures are vendor claims. Campaign credits count against the same pool as contact enrichment, so heavy ad use reduces credits available for contact reveals.

**Who benefits most:** marketing teams running account-based programs that already invest in display ads.

### 6. MCP Server and APIs
<Image src={pic4} alt="What Is Leadfeeder?" class="prose-img" />

**What it does.** Announced on 24 June 2026 and available from 25 June for accounts with API access, the Leadfeeder MCP server connects your account to Claude, ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot as a custom connector. It exposes about 40 actions across finding companies and contacts, enriching records, building and tagging lists, and checking usage. You sign in with OAuth, and any action that changes data asks for confirmation. Separate APIs convert companies to IP ranges and turn IP addresses into firmographic data.

**Why it matters.** Instead of opening a dashboard, a rep can ask which good-fit companies visited this week, request an account brief and save the strongest matches to a list, all inside the assistant.

**Limits.** The vendor's announcement does not name the plan tier required, only API access. The features page labels the server as beta, while the announcement does not, so expect some change.

**Who benefits most:** teams that already work in an AI assistant and want signal and enrichment without switching tools.

## Security & Privacy

Leadfeeder publishes more here than many tools in this category, so we list what is documented and what is not.

### What is documented

- **Certifications:** the security page says the information security and privacy programs are built on ISO 27001 and ISO 27701, and points to a Compliance Hub and Legal Kit. Ask for the actual certificates if your procurement team needs them.
- **Hosting:** infrastructure runs on Amazon Web Services inside the EU and EEA. The GDPR page names Ireland.
- **Encryption:** data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
- **Operations:** 24/7 runtime monitoring, web application firewall, DDoS protection, intrusion detection, separate production and test environments, and no customer personal data in testing.
- **Vulnerability management:** automated scanning and a private bug bounty on HackerOne.
- **People and resilience:** NDAs for all staff, annual security training, automated backups with defined recovery targets, a public status page and cyber insurance.
- **DPA:** a data processing agreement is available, concluded automatically when you share personal data through the site script or CRM connector. An EEA-only version and a version with European Standard Contractual Clauses for the US entity are linked.
- **Governance:** a named group data protection officer, a blocklist and objection handling, and a check against do-not-call lists in many countries.

**Legal basis.** Leadfeeder relies on legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR and says external counsel helped with the balancing test. It states that customers must run their own assessment. That matters because of a point raised by a German-law review: under the German TDDDG, tracking for identification may need consent before the script runs, which shrinks the audience you can identify to visitors who accept the relevant cookie category. This is one reviewer's reading, not legal advice, so speak to counsel.

### What we could not find

- No SOC 2 report mentioned
- No description of SSO or two-factor authentication for the app
- No penetration-testing statement (a bug bounty is listed instead)
- No sub-processor list on the security page, although the GDPR page says agreements are in place with all sub-processors

**Verdict on security.** Strong for a tool of this size and a good fit for EU-based buyers. Confirm SSO and certificate details in writing if you work in a regulated sector.

### How identified companies and credits work

Leadfeeder prices by two quotas: the number of unique companies identified per month, and credits for enrichment and activation actions.

- **Identified company:** a unique company resolved from your traffic in one month. Repeat visits count once, but a company with several office locations may appear once per location.
- **Credits:** used for CRM pushes, contact enrichment and campaigns. Identifying companies does not use credits.
- **Expiry:** standard plan credits do not roll over at the end of the contract period. One-off top-ups, sold in blocks of 100 to 5,000, are valid for two years.
- **Users:** unlimited on every plan, sharing one credit pool.

**Example 1: band pricing on Discover**

| Companies identified per month | Monthly cost (annual billing) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Up to 50 | €79 |
| 51 to 100 | €109 |
| 101 to 200 | €139 |
| 201 to 400 | €179 |
| 401 to 500 | €249 |
| 501 to 1,000 | €309 |

A site that identifies 450 companies a month lands in the €249 band, or about €2,988 over twelve months. Cross 500 and the cost rises to €309, so check your Lite data first to see which band you are likely to fall into.

**Example 2: annual versus monthly.** Discover at the entry level costs €79 × 12 = €948 on annual billing, against €113 × 12 = €1,356 month to month. That is about €408 extra a year, or roughly 43% more, for flexibility. Activate shows the same pattern: €4,428 annually versus €6,324 monthly.

**Example 3: what credits buy on Activate.** The entry Activate configuration (1,000 companies and 200 credits) costs €369 a month. Using the credit rules reported by one reviewer, 200 credits unlock either 200 contacts with email and phone, or about 1,000 email-only contacts. Top up to 500 credits and the same tier is €419. Heavy outbound teams will watch this pool more than the company count.

**Example 4: the jump from Discover to Activate.** Moving from Discover's entry price of €79 to Activate's €369 is a 4.7 times increase. One reviewer calls this the key decision point. Teams that only need identification and CRM sync can stay on Discover. Teams that need verified contacts, intent filters or campaigns have to make the larger step.

### Where costs can creep up

- **Volume bands.** More traffic means more identified companies and a higher band.
- **Credit depletion.** Campaigns and enrichment share one pool, and plan credits do not roll over.
- **Multiple domains.** Several reviewers describe pricing as complicated for more than one website, and one source says each extra site is billed separately. Confirm before adding domains.
- **Renewal changes.** Some users report price increases of 20% to 40% after the 2026 rebrand. The vendor says prices stayed the same at the rebrand, so ask for the renewal price in writing.

### Which plan should you choose?

- **Just exploring:** Lite, to see real identified companies from your traffic.
- **Marketing team that wants alerts and CRM sync:** Discover.
- **Sales team running outbound or account-based programs:** Activate.
- **Revenue team with high volume:** Scale, mainly for exports, CRM refresh and the success manager.
