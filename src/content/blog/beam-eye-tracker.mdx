### Beam Eye Tracker vs TrackIR

<Image src={beamVsPic1} alt="Beam Eye Tracker vs TrackIR" class="prose-img" />

TrackIR is one of the better-known dedicated head-tracking solutions for PC gaming.

The fundamental difference is simple:
Beam uses a camera and software. TrackIR uses dedicated infrared hardware.

TrackIR uses an infrared sensor and an emitter attached to a headset or cap. Beam does not require a physical emitter or tracking clip.

**Where TrackIR Has an Advantage**
TrackIR remains attractive for gamers who want dedicated hardware and highly responsive head tracking.
Because the system is designed specifically around infrared tracking hardware, it can provide a very consistent tracking experience without depending on a normal webcam.
This can be especially appealing for serious simulation players who spend hundreds of hours in games such as flight and racing simulators.

**Where Beam Has an Advantage**
Beam is much easier to transport and set up.
You can use the webcam already built into your computer or an external webcam. You can also use a smartphone as the camera.
That means there is no infrared camera to mount and no physical emitter to attach to your headset or cap.

The cost difference is also significant. Beam’s standalone license is currently $29.99, whereas TrackIR requires dedicated hardware.

If you specifically want eye tracking without buying a dedicated eye-tracking device, Beam has a clear advantage. If your priority is dedicated infrared head tracking and you do not mind wearing an emitter, TrackIR remains a strong option.

### Use Cases: Flight Sims, Racing, and Streaming

**Which Is Better for Flight Simulators?**
Flight simulation is one of the most obvious use cases for head tracking. Being able to look around the cockpit naturally can make it easier to:
- Check mirrors
- Look toward instruments
- Scan the environment
- Look into turns
- Keep your hands on the controls

Beam supports hundreds of PC games, including flight, space, racing, trucking and combat simulations. TrackIR is also a long-established option for simulation players and is specifically known for dedicated head tracking. Tobii is another strong choice, particularly for users who want both head and eye tracking.

*Best Choice for Flight Sims:*
- **Choose Beam if:** you want low upfront cost and do not want dedicated hardware.
- **Choose TrackIR if:** dedicated infrared head tracking is your priority.
- **Choose Tobii if:** you want dedicated eye and head tracking and are comfortable paying more.

**Which Is Better for Racing Games?**
Racing games can benefit significantly from head tracking because the driver can naturally look toward corners and surroundings while keeping both hands on the wheel. Beam’s 6DoF tracking can be used independently or together with eye tracking.
For casual or intermediate sim racers, the low price can make Beam particularly attractive. For competitive users who want a dedicated hardware solution, TrackIR may still be preferable. Tobii is worth considering if eye tracking is important to your setup.

**Which Is Better for Streamers?**
Beam has some features that are particularly interesting for content creators. Its eye-tracking overlay can show viewers where you are looking while playing. Beam states that the overlay works with OBS and Streamlabs.
This can create additional visual information for flight simulation streams, racing streams, FPS content, tutorials, gaming analysis, and YouTube videos. Beam’s community profile system can also allow creators to share their tracking settings with viewers.

### Hardware Dependencies and Privacy

**What About Gaming Without a Webcam?**
This is where dedicated hardware solutions have an obvious advantage. Beam fundamentally depends on a camera. If you do not have a suitable webcam or smartphone camera, you will need to obtain one.
The good news is that Beam states a standard 720p webcam can be enough for basic use at 30 FPS, making the barrier to entry relatively low. Dedicated hardware trackers do not have this dependency.

**Is Webcam Tracking Accurate Enough?**
For normal gaming and immersive camera control, webcam tracking can be an attractive alternative to dedicated hardware. However, dedicated infrared systems can still make sense for users who prioritize highly specialized tracking performance.
Beam itself recommends trying the product before buying hardware, offering a free Steam demo and a 14-day money-back guarantee for the standalone version.

**Privacy: Does Beam Store Biometric Data?**
Privacy can be an important consideration when using a camera-based tracking system. Beam’s current website states that the app does not generate or store biometric data. It also states that processing is local. This is worth considering if you prefer a tracking solution that processes information locally.
